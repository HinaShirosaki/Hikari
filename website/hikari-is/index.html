Hikari is

One request. Connected work.

Ask Hikari to work across your protocols, samples, notebook, and papers. It finds the context to help write methods, prepare pages, locate materials, and analyze results.

  • One conversation across your lab records
  • Write, find, explain, plot with your context
  • Review first before saving drafts

Design your cloning plan

Make a sequence edit, compare cloning routes, and get primers for the job. Review each primer’s properties, then copy the sequences or export a list for your order.

  • 7 cloning routes, including KLD and Gibson
  • Check primers: length, Tm, and GC content
  • Ready to order: copy sequences or export for IDT

Build a protein. Prepare the sequence.

Assemble proteins, tags, linkers, and cleavage sites into one chain. Review the assembled amino acid sequence and build coding DNA with your chosen codon usage. Copy either sequence into an ordering platform for gene synthesis.

  • Build your chain from protein blocks
  • Generate coding DNA with your codon usage
  • Copy protein or DNA for gene synthesis

Bench calculations, on paper

Enter three known values to calculate the fourth, or leave two values for Hikari to write out the remaining arithmetic. Export the table with your notebook PDF and print it. At the bench, a lab calculator is all you need to finish the calculations—no return to the computer.

  • Calculate in place in your notebook
  • Print your worksheet with the notebook PDF
  • Finish at the bench with a lab calculator

Reuse a method. Record the run.

Start from a saved protocol and fill in the details for this run. Steps, sample links, and observations stay together in a notebook page, even when you later update the library.

  • Reuse a saved method for each run
  • Capture the details: samples, values, and notes
  • Preserve each run when the library changes

Repeat the process. Trace the run.

Turn a process you repeat into a reusable workflow. Each run keeps its own progress, results, and notebook links, so you can see what happened and what comes next.

  • One template for repeated runs
  • Track progress through steps and branches
  • Keep the trail: results and notebook links

Find every sample

Know what a sample is, where it’s stored, and which experiments used it. Keep plasmids, proteins, cell lines, and other materials together, with records linked to your notebook.

  • Find the well, find your sample
  • Know the sample: concentration, batch, and notes
  • Connect your records with CSV and experiment links

Your workspace. Your tools.

Shape Hikari around the way you work. Add a plugin when you need something new, and keep your everyday research in the same workspace.

  • Add the tools you need through plugins
  • Check permissions before enabling a plugin
  • Grow your setup as your work evolves

Free and open source.Optional AI requires a ChatGPT plan with Codex CLI access.

macOS · Open Terminal, paste the command below, then press Return.

curl -fsSL https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@hinashirosaki/hikari/install.sh | bash

Install guide

  1. Open the terminal

    On macOS, press Command–Space, type Terminal, and press Return. On Windows, press the Windows key, type PowerShell, and press Enter.

  2. Run the command

    Copy the install command on the page, paste it into the terminal, and press Enter or Return.

  3. Wait for installation

    Keep the terminal window open until the command finishes. The first run may take several minutes. The app is saved in hikari-out, inside the folder where you ran the command.

  4. Open Hikari

    Open the output folder when the command finishes. On macOS, move Hikari.app to Applications and open it. On Windows, run HikariSetup.exe, then open Hikari from the Start menu.

Good to know

Practical answers,
before you begin.

A few details about bringing Hikari to your bench.

Which systems are supported?
Hikari runs on macOS and Windows.
Where does my data live?
Your records and files are saved in a folder you choose on your computer. Read about storage.
Do I need AI to use Hikari?
No. The optional agent needs a ChatGPT plan with Codex CLI access. Prompts, attachments, and record content used by the agent are sent to OpenAI.
Is Hikari released?
Hikari is free and open source, and currently in beta. See releases for the latest version.
Where can I get help?
Start with the user guide, or report a problem on GitHub Issues.

Hikari is

Your ChatGPT plan. No API key.

Hikari’s optional agent runs through Codex CLI with a ChatGPT plan that includes CLI access. Prompts, attachments, and the record content it uses are sent to OpenAI for processing. Connect it in Hikari Settings; your plan’s usage limits apply.

  • Free and open source Hikari desktop app
  • No account needed for core lab tools
  • No Hikari subscription for the optional agent

Write and polish protocols

Describe a method, paste one from a paper, or attach notes and images, and Hikari drafts a structured protocol. Polish rewrites a rough draft in clear lab wording, side by side with your original.

  • Start with what you have: notes, images, or a PDF
  • Polish the wording, keep your steps and fields
  • Compare side by side before applying

Explain what you select

Select a reagent or term in a protocol or notebook page. Explain selection describes its role in that step, and Find suppliers lists products to compare. The answer stays on the text for next time.

  • Understand the step with answers in context
  • Compare suppliers and prices
  • Keep the answer on the record for next time

Find, save, and ask about papers

Search for related work and save the PDFs to your paper library. If a publisher blocks the download, Hikari opens its page so you can click Download. Then ask about the paper you are reading, or turn its methods into a protocol draft.

  • Build your library with saved PDFs
  • Ask as you read about the open paper
  • From methods to draft in your protocol library

Plot by asking

With an assay open, ask for the chart you need. Hikari reads the plate and its results and draws an interactive chart in the analysis panel, in the same style as Hikari’s built-in charts.

  • Use your plate data from the open assay
  • Explore, then export charts as PNG or SVG
  • Keep a consistent style with built-in charts

Review notebook and protocol drafts

Drafted notebook pages, additions to existing pages, and new protocols wait for your review. Approve a draft to add it to your records, or reject it.

  • Draft notebook pages or add to existing ones
  • Prepare protocols from chat or a paper
  • You decide: approve or reject each draft

    Hikari plugins

    Tools for your lab.

    Add the tools your lab needs, right inside Hikari.

    Figura

    Generate artwork with AI and combine it with editable diagrams, plots, and labels to compose scientific figures. Reuse saved components, then export as SVG, PNG, or PowerPoint.

    View plugin ↗

    Install a plugin.

    Open Settings → Plugins in Hikari, then choose either option.

    From GitHub

    Enter the plugin’s repository URL and click Install from GitHub. Requires Git.

    For Figura: https://github.com/HinaShirosaki/Figura

    From a folder

    Click Add Plugin Folder and open the plugin folder containing plugin.json.

    Review the plugin’s access, enable it, then click Reload App. Open the plugin from the dock or More menu.

    Build your own.

    Make a tool for your workflow with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Give it a workspace in Hikari and connect it to the notebook.
